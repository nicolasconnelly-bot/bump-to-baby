Medical and safety disclaimer

Updated 2026-10-14. Bump to Baby is a shopping and planning guide. It is not medical care, not a car-seat inspection, and not dosing advice.

Not medical advice

Nothing here diagnoses, treats, or tells you what your baby needs. Talk with your prenatal clinician and your baby's pediatrician about feeding, illness, medicine, and sleep. If something feels urgent, contact them or emergency services rather than a website.

Safe sleep

The general notes on this site follow a simple pattern: alone, on the back, on a firm flat mattress in a crib, bassinet, or play yard made for sleep, with nothing else in that space. No bumpers, pillows, quilts, stuffed animals, or sleep positioners. A swaddle or wearable sleep sack is the cover, and swaddling stops when a baby shows signs of rolling. Many families room-share for the first months, with the baby in a separate sleep space. A bouncer or swing is not a sleep space. This is general information. Ask your pediatrician what applies to your baby.

Car seats

Most hospitals expect a rear-facing car seat for the ride home. Buy it new, or use one only if you know its history, including whether it has been in a crash. Check the expiration date. Have the install checked by a certified child passenger safety technician or a free check through a hospital or fire station. The "inch test" mentioned in week 12 is a general reminder, not a substitute for that check. Airline rules differ; ask the airline before you fly. The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration publishes car seat information at nhtsa.gov.

Medicine

Infant acetaminophen is on the gear list because families are often told to have some on hand. This site does not say whether your baby should have it, and it does not give doses. Ask your pediatrician before you use it. The same is true for saline drops and any other health item named here.

Recalls and standards

Cribs should meet current safety standards and have fixed sides. Mattresses should be firm and tight-fitting. Anchor dressers to the wall. Check recalls before you buy or reuse gear. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission lists recalls at cpsc.gov/Recalls.

Feeding

Insurance may cover a breast pump in the United States. Formula choices and breastfeeding problems belong with your clinician or a lactation consultant. Buy one small can of formula first if you are using it. Store-brand formulas sold in the U.S. have to meet FDA standards; that is a regulatory fact, not a ranking of brands.
